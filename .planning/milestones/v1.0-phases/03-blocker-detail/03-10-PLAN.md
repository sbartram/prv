## Flagged decisions (carried, not silently dropped)

- **D-10 label superseded by the user's UAT decision.** CONTEXT D-10 writes the fold line as "… +N more passing". In 03-UAT.md the user marked WR-04 "fix before ship", and gap G-03-6-WR04 lists "Reword fold line to '+N more passing or skipped'" as missing. This plan follows the newer user decision. The rest of D-10 holds: the order, the faint skipped/neutral checks, e to expand, and failing, cancelled and running always listed. D-10 says nothing about unknown checks; they are now always listed too.
- **The detail pause now uses the list path's reset margin.** NextDelay waits until the reset time plus 1 s (resetWait). ServerWait reuses resetWait, so a detail error with a reset 10 minutes ahead now pauses until reset + 1 s, not until the exact reset. Two existing subtests of TestDetailRateLimitExtendsPause ("a reset time" and "a refresh result does not end the pause") change their expected value from reset to reset + 1 s. That is a deliberate expectation change, not a regression.
- **Retry-After now wins over a reset time on the detail path**, as it already does in NextDelay ("Order: a Retry-After wins; then a known reset time"). Today the detail path prefers the reset.
- **No fallback to the snapshot's reset time.** When the error has no Retry-After and no reset after now, NextDelay would try `m.snap.RateLimit.ResetAt` before its floor. ServerWait goes straight to RateLimitFloor (60 s). That keeps it a pure function of the response. The result stays inside [MinServerWait, MaxServerWait] for any configured refresh interval, while NextDelay's failure fallback grows with a long interval.
- NextDelay itself is not changed. ServerWait reuses its private clamp and resetWait helpers, so the bounds cannot drift. TestServerWait cross-checks it against NextDelay.
